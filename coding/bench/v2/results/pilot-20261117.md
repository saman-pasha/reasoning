## What the pilot says

- **The held-out split changed the score.** Three cells passed every
  repair case and failed the held-out set: `lcs/plain` trials 1 and 3, and
  `mvccs_acid/plain` trial 2. A repair-only benchmark would have counted
  them as passes.
- **`lcs/derive` is the clearest gain.** It passed 3/3 at pass@1, while
  `lcs/plain` passed 1/3; the repair cases alone do not distinguish a
  subsequence from a substring, and the held-out set does.
- **`stable_sort` costs repair, not correctness.** `plain` passed 3/3 on
  the first attempt. `derive` needed two attempts twice and one once, so
  its final score is 3/3 but its token cost is 5.8 times `plain`'s. Three
  trials is too few to call that a derive penalty.
- **`mvccs_acid` is the budget problem.** It solved 0/6 and consumed
  140 704 of the 152 497 tokens. `plain` failed repair in trials 1 and 3
  and passed repair but failed held-out in trial 2; `derive` failed repair
  in all three. The task's three-call budget and 5000 ms case timeout need
  review before the full run, not a larger N.

These are pilot observations, not estimates: N=3 per cell, and no
confidence interval is computed yet. The next run should fix the
`mvccs_acid` budget or task, then repeat the pilot before sizing the full
matrix.
